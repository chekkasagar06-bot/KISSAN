About

Agriculture is the backbone of India. Farmers grow food crops such as rice, wheat, maize, and cotton, which are essential for the country's food security and economy. Agriculture provides employment to millions of people and supports many industries. Our website provides information about different crops, cultivation methods, modern farming practices, irrigation techniques, fertilizers, pest and disease management, and harvesting methods. It also helps farmers learn about sustainable agriculture, organic farming, and the latest agricultural technologies to improve crop yield and productivity.

Our Crops

Rice

Rice is the staple food crop of India and requires plenty of water.

Season

Kharif Season (June–November) is the main rice-growing season in India.

Common Diseases

  • Leaf Blast
  • Brown Spot
  • Bacterial Leaf Blight
  • Sheath Blight

Wheat

Wheat is grown during winter and is used for flour and bread.

Season

Rabi Season (October–December sowing, March–April harvesting).

Common Diseases

  • Leaf Rust
  • Stem Rust
  • Stripe Rust (Yellow Rust)
  • Powdery Mildew
  • Loose Smut

Maize

Maize is used as food, animal feed, and in many industries.

Season

Kharif Season (June–July sowing, September–October harvesting).

Common Diseases

  • Leaf Blight
  • Common Rust
  • Downy Mildew
  • Stalk Rot
  • Maydis Leaf Blight

Cotton

Cotton is an important cash crop used to make clothes and textiles.

Season

Kharif Season (April–June sowing, October–January harvesting).

Common Diseases

  • Bacterial Blight
  • Fusarium Wilt
  • Alternaria Leaf Spot
  • Root Rot
  • Grey Mildew

Tomato

Tomato is one of the most widely grown vegetable crops in India. It is rich in vitamins and is used in salads, curries, sauces, and ketchup.

Season

Kharif (June–July), Rabi (October–November), and Summer (January–February).

Common Diseases

  • Early Blight
  • Late Blight
  • Leaf Curl Virus
  • Bacterial Wilt
  • Septoria Leaf Spot
